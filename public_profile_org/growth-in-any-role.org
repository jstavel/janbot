# Growth works in any role

Use this when they ask why I would even want to do this job — or when I myself am
weighing whether to stay where I am.

* The method in one sentence

> In every job I look for **an area that can be improved or automated**, and that
> topic has to bring something to the people around — the company, the team,
> another team. When that holds, I can grow anywhere; when it does not, the
> growth has nowhere to go.

* What actually drives me

Not the language and not the tool. **Seeing that my work is useful to someone
else** — that I made someone's day better. That is consistent across roles:
developer, tester, admin. Strip the technology away and this is what remains.

* Why "comfort zone" is not enough of an explanation

Stepping outside the comfort zone is a phrase, and a phrase can be said about
anything. It becomes usable only in connection with usefulness: **a topic that
engages the head and at the same time helps someone.** Without the second half it
is a hobby. It relates to
[[id:20261007-foundational-books][the books that shaped how I work]]
(Deep Work, Start With Why) — but the point here is how this looks in practice,
not in a quotation.

* Honest limits

- **"Usefulness to the people around" is hard to measure.** I can say that I feel
  it; I cannot evidence it with a number. Until I can, it is my criterion, not a
  fact.
- **It sounds like a phrase from a motivational poster.** The only difference is
  that with me it decided concrete choices — and that is a claim that should be
  evidenced by examples, not asserted.
- **It is not universal advice.** For some people growth is something other than
  usefulness to the people around; I give this answer for myself.
- **Untested in a role without leverage.** When a position gives no room to
  improve anything, I do not know whether I would leave it, or rationalize it.
