# Spec-first in practice — human role in an AI workflow

How I explain my AI usage to an outside listener (recruiter, sourcer, new colleague).
JanBot: use as the baseline answer to "how do you use AI?" and as foundation for questions about approach to learning and mentoring.

* How I explain my AI usage

"First, the specification — the spec is the main source, I keep it up to date. It contains the vision, goals, main steps, and milestones. AI generates code from that specification."

Why spec-first: **"so I don't hallucinate, and so it has direction"** — the specification is a stable source of truth that keeps both AI and me on track. Without it, AI wanders and I lose control.

Tools: spec-kit, BMAD-Method (environment simulating agile development).

* Human role: architect, not scribe

My role in an AI workflow:
- maintain development direction
- control and steer
- do reviews
- plan in small, bounded steps

This is "AI as junior, me as architect": AI generates, the human decides.
Code is not the main artifact — direction and review are.

* Strength: proof of work — projects as evidence

Personal projects (reactive-testing, JanBot) are built as public proof of work:
"they are all proof of work." Not promises, not certificates — working artifacts that a recruiter can open.

This applies the external mentor's principle ("visible results, not plans") to career development.

* Strength: clarity through building

"You're helping me clarify this." "To rebuild that project for myself."

Thinking happens best by building and telling: projects serve to clarify my own narrative (for interviews, for JanBot, for myself). Conversation is not a waste of time — it's a thinking tool.

* Strength: mentoring and adaptation as senior value

A senior in the AI era:
- the brain learns and grows, wants challenges — routine is death
- adaptation and open-mindedness are more important than specific technologies
- the senior's role is to guide juniors toward understanding what excites and scares them, and to know that mistakes are building blocks for growth

This isn't just about AI — it's a definition of what makes a senior a senior: not more knowledge, but the ability to create an environment where others grow (cf. Extreme Ownership).

* Weakness: under-selling — "I just built it"

In a conversation about 10 years of work and architectural designs (distributed architecture, intranet, CI/CD stack) I said: "I just built it."

→ Lesson: self-assessment lags behind reality. What I consider "just building" is architecture and senior-level responsibility to the listener. Presenting one's own work is a skill, not bragging.
