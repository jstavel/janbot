# Remote work by conviction — 15 years of home office, and what meetings cannot replace

Not "I tolerate remote" but "I have practised it for 15 years and know
exactly what it can and cannot do for a team." Use when a role asks
about hybrid / remote expectations.

* The position

- **15 years of working from home.** Remote is not an adaptation; it is
  the default mode of work.
- Routine collaboration — standups, alignment meetings — works fully
  virtual. Coming to an office to attend those is superfluous.

* What the office is actually for

The one thing virtual meetings do not replace: **the unstructured
personal contact** — people getting to know each other, sharing moments,
being together without an agenda. That is what builds the safety and
openness out of which people dare to make mistakes and collaborate.
So the position on hybrid: yes to in-person — **but only when it is
truly personal time**, not a commute for meetings that work better
remotely anyway.

* Flow as a working condition

Current personal flow practice: mornings start by taking the goats to
the pasture, then working — recently a six-hour uninterrupted session,
no lunch, deep-flow quality. The environment is chosen deliberately:
calm animals nearby, an uncalming presence, no interruptions. Flow is
treated as a design problem of the environment, not an accident.
