# Closing the drift — an onboarding document, a weekly block, a daily ritual

Use when asked how I keep up with my own AI-assisted work, or how a project is
supposed to be handed over. The three instruments below are one method: **the
drift is closed on a calendar, not on demand.**

* 1. The onboarding document — the compressed map

Joining a project means closing the gap between my knowledge and the project's.
The instrument is **one dense onboarding document**, not a pile of tickets and
not a Q&A bank:

- Q&A and quizzes are for **real newcomers** — someone meeting the domain for the
  first time needs the gradual, question-shaped path.
- For the owner of an AI-assisted project the need is different: a **compressed
  document** that can be read step by step and checked against reality ("do I
  understand this, can I do this?").
- AI writes and re-compresses it; the document **grows as skills and knowledge
  accumulate**. It is a living artifact, not a one-off handover pack.

The value is in the compression: a map dense enough to be re-read, short enough
to be re-read at all.

* 2. The weekly block — the end of the week is not development time

One block per week is reserved and **no development happens in it**. The block
closes the week: unfinished work is closed or explicitly parked, and the drift is
reduced on four axes —

- knowledge,
- experience (what was actually tried),
- the agentic workflow,
- the agentic team.

The point is the *reservation*: if closing is what remains after development, it
never happens. The week is a batch, and a batch needs a commit point.

* 3. The daily close — name the new knowledge, try one piece by hand

The weekly model has an honest weakness: drift accumulates faster than a weekly
close can absorb it. The refinement is a **daily** close:

1. at the end of the day, ask the agent: *what new knowledge appeared today?*
2. then close **both axes**, one item each — they close differently:
   - **knowledge**: take one decision and defend it out loud — the decision, the
     rejected alternative, what breaks if it changes (see
     [[id:20261018-knowledge-drift][knowledge drift]]),
   - **procedure**: take one command from the runbook and **run it by hand** —
     reading it does not count (see [[id:20261018-procedure-drift][procedure drift]]),
3. the day is then closed: no open loop carried into the night.

Two reasons, and the second is not a productivity argument: it closes the day
**emotionally and mentally**. An unclosed day is an open loop.

The two axes are separate steps because they fail differently: a decision can be
*known* and still not *defensible*, and a command can be *readable* and still not
*executable* by the person reading it.

* Honest limit — the cadence may still lose

The author states plainly that the drift between project knowledge and practical
skill is **already a month deep**, so a daily close will not necessarily keep
up. The hypothesis is that a shorter cadence at least stops the debt from
growing silently; it is an experiment, not a verified method. What is not in
doubt is the direction: **closing must be scheduled**, because unscheduled
learning is precisely how the gap accumulates.
