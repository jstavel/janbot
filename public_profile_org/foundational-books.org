* Foundational books — the framework behind the work

Eight books form a coherent professional philosophy that guides every
aspect of how this person works, leads, and grows. The framework:

1. **David Allen — Getting Things Done (GTD)**
   *"Mind like water — the mind is not a warehouse."*
   Applied through Org-mode: inbox capture, projects, horizons, weekly review.
   GTD is the architecture of his thinking.

2. **Carol Dweck — Growth Mindset**
   *"It's happening for me to grow."*
   Every challenge is an opportunity. The Kraken rejection → feedback → better profile.

3. **Cal Newport — Deep Work**
   *"The mind finds joy when working on something useful for yourself and others."*
   AI as a junior (OpenCode), himself as an architect. Deep work on specs, ADRs, Clojure.

4. **Simon Sinek — Start With Why**
   Purpose first, then method, then result. Career change 2026 was about meaning, not just a job.

5. **Simon Sinek — Leaders Eat Last**
   Safe environment and trust in teams. Applied as a mentor — listening, finding strengths,
   creating psychological safety.

6. **Jocko Willink — Extreme Ownership**
   - **UP:** Clean results — ensure manager understands what we're doing.
   - **DOWN:** Create an environment where a junior can grow.
   Applied at Red Hat: not just "helping" but taking ownership of another person's growth space.

7. **Tiago Forte — Building a Second Brain**
   Collect, organize, distill knowledge. This very corpus project is the implementation of
   that system — memories → distillation → publication.

8. **James Clear — Atomic Habits**
   Willpower is overrated. Attach a small new step to an existing routine.
   Applied through GTD: inbox capture, weekly review, project planning as a chain of habits.

**The resulting philosophy:** organized mind (GTD) + growth through challenge (Dweck) +
focused creation (Newport) + meaningful direction (Sinek) + full accountability (Willink) +
systematic knowledge management (Forte) + habit design (Clear).
