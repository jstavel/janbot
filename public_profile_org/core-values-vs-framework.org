# Core values vs. competency framework — the spirit travels person to person

A reflection on why core values are the strongest force in a company —
and why a competency framework is only a tool in their service. Use
when discussing company culture, seniority, or how values outlive any
documented framework.

* The argument

- **Company values exist to awaken willingness** — loyalty and the
  desire to do one's best for the company. Willingness is tied to
  **freedom**: the person themselves feels and sees what is best for
  the company, what they can contribute, and how — as long as it is
  aligned with the long-term goals and commitments of the team.
- A company also has an elaborate **competency framework** — a large
  table describing what a Senior / Principal engineer is: cross-team
  influence, go-to person, the one people ask for advice.
- The reflection: **a framework is just a tool.** The spirit of the
  company — freedom, commitment, courage, accountability — is not in
  the table. It travels **from person to person**: through leaders who
  carry it and apply it when judging work.

* Why core values are the stronger force

1. **Values generate behaviour; frameworks only classify it.** A
   competency framework measures reach — cross-team influence, being a
   go-to person. It can describe *what seniority looks like*, but it
   cannot *cause* anyone to behave with freedom, commitment, courage or
   accountability. Values do exactly that: they tell people why to
   reach beyond their immediate scope at all.
2. **Values are portable; frameworks are situational.** Values travel
   person to person and stay alive even when the org chart changes. A
   framework is tied to a specific table, version, and interpretation —
   and dies the moment the interpretation drifts.
3. **Values are self-fulfilling when leaders carry them.** The spirit
   is not in the document. It is in leaders who *apply* the values when
   judging work — freedom, commitment, courage, accountability. When a
   leader judges through values, the framework becomes a helpful map.
   When a leader judges through the framework alone, the values become
   marketing and the table becomes the real culture.
4. **Because values are the stronger force, align on them first.**
   When a framework's boxes and the company's stated values point in
   different directions, the productive move is to name it early and
   explicitly ask which one governs the decision — so that months of
   work are measured by the values, not by an interpretation of a
   table.

* Personal note — values as a compass

When the author loses direction — searching for the next goal, unsure
what matters — recalling the company's core values is what reorients
him. The values work as a compass: they don't prescribe a specific
next step, but they make the *right kind* of step visible (freedom to
choose it, courage to reach beyond scope, commitment to the team's
long-term goals, accountability for the result). That is practical
evidence of the argument above: values generate behaviour even when
no framework is consulted.

The guard against reading this as stubbornness: daily commitments
come first — the team's plan is done honestly, every day. The "why"
is not a veto on the plan; it is what gives the plan meaning and is
protection against burnout. Values inside commitments (freedom +
courage), commitments without values (compliance, burnout), values
without commitments (stubbornness, air bridges). Only together is
it full engagement.

* Lessons

1. **A competency framework measures reach, not spirit.** Seniority
   defined as "cross-team influence, go-to person" says nothing about
   *why* someone reaches across teams — only the values do.
2. **Culture is transmitted person to person**, not by document. The
   people who carry the values are the actual culture.
3. **When values and framework collide, name the collision early** —
   ask explicitly which one governs the decision. Because values are
   the stronger and more durable force, they should win.
