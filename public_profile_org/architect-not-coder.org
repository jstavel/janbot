# Hired for judgement, not for typing — and saying so honestly

Use when asked about interviewing, about what a senior developer is worth in the
AI era, or about how to present a skill that was built with agents.

* What the interview actually tested

A final round for a Clojure role. The hands-on part was deliberately small — a
project skeleton and one small problem — and the question that carried the signal
was not *"write this"* but *"what are the trade-offs between the libraries you
would reach for, and why"*.

No request for a large amount of code. The interviewer was not measuring typing
speed; he was measuring whether the candidate **knows the ecosystem's
trade-offs and can reason as a designer**.

* The reading — and the correction

The first reading was tempting: *"maybe I do not need to be able to code it any
more, only to know about it as the designer."* That is half true and it is
exactly the half that is dangerous.

The corrected reading, kept as the lesson: **the centre of gravity moves from
syntax to judgement, but it does not move to zero.** Trade-off knowledge without
the ability to implement is opinion; implementation without trade-off knowledge
is typing. A senior is expected to hold both.

* Where honesty bites

The portfolio contains TypeScript projects that were built with agents. There is
a real asymmetry between

- what the artifact demonstrates (architecture, specification, verification
  discipline), and
- what the hands can do unaided (not yet much).

Presenting that as TypeScript expertise would be an **over-claim manufactured by
the workflow**. The rule from [[id:20261018-substitutable-ai][AI must be
substitutable — and so must I]] applies directly: if I cannot reproduce it
without the agents, I do not claim it.

* The decision

Two options were open: fill the gap first, or go to market and declare it.

**Chosen: go to market and declare it.** The reason is contextual, not noble —
a long period without work makes visibility the more urgent need. A targeted
course to raise the level runs in parallel; it is not a precondition for
speaking to the market.

How the declaration sounds:

> *"TypeScript is a new skill for me. I build with agents and I am learning it
> deliberately — I can show you the architecture and the verification, and I
> will tell you where the language itself is still ahead of me."*

The honest version is also the stronger version: it demonstrates that the
candidate distinguishes **what he designed** from **what he can reproduce**, which
is precisely the judgement the interview was testing for.

* Honest limit

The specific library trade-offs were not recalled in the interview itself — the
gap was named by the interviewer, not by the candidate. Recorded here as a
learning item, not as a strength.
